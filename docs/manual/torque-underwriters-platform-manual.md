## 1. What Torque Underwriters is

Torque Underwriters is a **Managing General Agent (MGA)** for luxury, exotic, and collector auto insurance, aimed at high-net-worth clients across (in v1) all 50 US states.

An MGA is an intermediary that has been *delegated underwriting authority* by a capacity provider — here, Lloyd's of London syndicates — under a **binding authority agreement** (a "binder"). Practically, that means Torque can do everything an insurer does at the front line — take applications, assess risk, price, decide, and issue policies — **but it does not carry the insurance risk on its own balance sheet.** The risk sits with the Lloyd's syndicates that back the binder. Torque earns its money through commission on the premium it generates and a **profit commission** if the book performs well.

The platform in this manual is the software that operationalizes that MGA: it takes in an application, enriches it, decides whether it can be auto-handled or must go to a human, prices what it's allowed to price, keeps every decision inside the legal boundary of what each state permits, and records everything in an audit-ready way.

Everything the platform does traces back to one of two masters: **the binder** (what the syndicates authorized) or **the regulator** (what each state's insurance department permits). If a behavior can't be traced to one of those, it doesn't belong in the system. That principle is borrowed directly from how a Lloyd's coverholder is expected to operate, and it is the reason the platform is structured around referral gates and a per-state compliance registry rather than a single global rating function.

## 2. The strategic thesis (why the machine is built the way it is)

The platform's architecture is a direct expression of the business bet. It's worth stating plainly, because it explains why so much effort goes into automation and cost discipline rather than into clever pricing.

**The bet:** insurance markets soften. Rates fall, commissions compress, and margins thin across a cycle. Most MGAs compete on price and relationships, and when the market softens their expense ratio eats them alive. Torque competes on **expense efficiency**: heavy automation makes the cost of writing and servicing a policy low enough that Torque stays profitable *after* rates soften and commissions compress — the point at which higher-cost competitors go red.

The critical nuance, and the most persuasive part of the story: **the MGA competes away expense and margin, never loss cost.** Loss cost — what claims actually cost — does not soften with the market. So the durable advantage is the cost structure, not underpricing risk. The moment a carrier program turns red on a softening market while a lean MGA is still profitable is the crux of the whole thesis.

Concretely, this is why the platform:

- automates intake, enrichment, referral gating, rating, and quoting so a clean risk needs no human touch;
- puts humans only where judgment or compliance genuinely requires them (the referral matrix);
- runs on commodity cloud infrastructure with no per-seat SaaS licensing (self-hosted Odoo, open-source stack);
- treats every decision as a recorded, replayable artifact so audit and regulatory cost stays low as the book scales.

## 4. Environments: production vs. demo

There are two environments, and keeping them straight matters because they hold different data and serve different purposes.

**Production** lives on the `main` branch and the production database. It carries the real platform build — ADRs 0001 through 0043 as shipped.

**Demo** lives on the `demo/investor-preview` branch, cut from `main`, and runs against a **separate database, `luxauto_demo`, on the same PostgreSQL server.** The separation is by `PGDATABASE` override, not by schema. This was a deliberate architectural choice: the platform's stored procedures are `SECURITY DEFINER` functions that pin `SET search_path = public`, so isolating the demo by schema would have required duplicating 30+ function definitions. A separate database gets clean isolation essentially for free.

The demo environment has four states onboarded (CA, NY, TX, FL) via the `onboard_state()` procedure, using **clearly synthetic rating data** — SERFF filing numbers prefixed `DEMO-SYNTHETIC-`, and the `verified_by` field explicitly labeled demo-only so no one can mistake it for filed data. Sample applications are seeded, and the referral matrix is exercised against them.

On top of the demo database sit three systemd services on the VM:

- **Dashboard exporter** — read-only; queries the demo DB and writes `snapshot.json`, the file the dashboard renders from. It never writes to the database.
- **Synthetic generator** — a preset-driven generator of synthetic submissions; normally left paused.
- **Demo control agent** — backs the control panel that drives the demo.

The dashboard and control panel are deployed to Azure Static Web Apps behind Entra auth, with Cloudflare CNAMEs pointing the friendly domains at them.

## 5. What happens that you can't see — the pipeline

This is the heart of Kent's question: *what is the machine doing between "an application arrives" and "a quote (or a referral) comes out"?* Almost all of it is invisible from the outside. Here is the sequence, in order.

**Stage 1 — Intake.** An application arrives conforming to the application schema (`luxury_auto_application_schema.json`). It carries the applicant, any additional drivers, one or more vehicles, the coverage requested, prior insurance, and 5-year claims history. Each application gets a unique ID and an initial status. Missing risk-critical fields are *not* silently defaulted — that's a referral condition (DH-04), not a guess.

**Stage 2 — Enrichment.** The raw application is augmented with external data the applicant didn't (or couldn't) provide: motor-vehicle record (MVR), VIN decode, title-history lookup, prior-carrier loss run, and household/sanctions screening. This enrichment is what makes the later referral checks meaningful — the system is comparing what was *declared* against what is *true*. (Several of these enrichment sources are still stubs pending vendor integration — see §13.)

**Stage 3 — Referral gating (before any pricing).** Every rule in the referral matrix is evaluated against the enriched application **before the rating engine runs.** This ordering is deliberate and mirrors how a Lloyd's coverholder gates pricing on aggregate/authority checks before touching a rating worksheet: you confirm the risk is yours to price and clean enough to price *before* you price it. A rule firing writes a `reason_code` to the application's decision log — unredacted — even when the ultimate outcome is "proceed." (Full detail in §6.)

**Stage 4 — Rating.** If the application clears the gates (or a human clears a referral), the rating engine runs the rating chain (§7), producing a technical premium and a gross written premium, staying strictly inside the state's permitted rating variables (§8).

**Stage 5 — Quote / decision.** The system emits either a quote or a routed referral. Either way, it attaches a **decision log** citing which permitted variables drove the price and which rules fired — the artifact that makes an adverse-action explanation or a market-conduct exam answerable later.

The invisible machinery underneath all of this is a set of PostgreSQL stored procedures (the `create_quote()` RPC and its supporting functions), running as `SECURITY DEFINER` so they execute with controlled, consistent privileges regardless of who calls them. The application layer never rewrites rating logic in app code — it calls into the database, which is the single source of truth for how a risk is priced and gated.

## 6. The referral matrix — the hard-stop layer

The referral matrix (`luxury_auto_referral_matrix.json`) is where *"the model isn't sure"* or *"the model is sure but the answer needs a human or a compliance check"* gets encoded as executable logic instead of being left to discretion under deadline pressure. It is the pipeline's hard-stop layer.

**The action taxonomy** — what "a rule fired" can mean:

| Action | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `AUTO_PROCEED` | No referral; rating runs normally |
| `AUTO_PROCEED_WITH_FLAG` | Rating runs, but the flag is attached for later audit sampling — non-blocking |
| `INFORMATION_REQUEST` | Pipeline halts and generates a specific itemized request back to the applicant/broker — a completeness gate, not yet a human referral |
| `MANUAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED` | Routed to a human underwriter before any quote is released |
| `MANUAL_REVIEW_SENIOR` | Requires a senior underwriter / second sign-off — highest-severity triggers |
| `DECLINE_RECOMMENDED` | Pipeline recommends decline with reason pre-filled, but a human confirms |
| `HARD_DECLINE_COMPLIANCE` | Sanctions/legal hits only — no human discretion; if it fires, the application cannot proceed |

**A deliberate design choice:** nothing in the matrix auto-declines purely on a model decision **except** `HARD_DECLINE_COMPLIANCE` (sanctions). An AI-driven decline with no human in the loop is exactly the fact pattern that draws NAIC Model Bulletin and state DFS/DOI scrutiny. `DECLINE_RECOMMENDED` keeps a human as the accountable decision-maker while still handing them a fully-reasoned output to confirm or override.

**The rule categories:**

- **Vehicle & valuation (VV-01…04)** — VIN-to-declared mismatch, undisclosed adverse title brand, stale/unsupported agreed value, undeclared modifications affecting value or risk.
- **Driver & household (DH-01…04)** — DUI within look-back, undisclosed household driver, suspended/revoked license, insufficient data to compute risk.
- **Account & loss (AL-01…02)** — adverse loss history, prior non-renewal/cancellation.
- **Coverage & pricing (CP-01…03)** — suspected undisclosed business use, authority-limit exceeded, rating-band exceeded.
- **Process & conduct (PC-01…04)** — garaging/mailing mismatch (rate evasion), sanctions hit, out-of-licensed-territory, unverified producer.

Every rule that can vary by state carries a `state_override_hook` that points into that state's rating-registry record, so a state can tighten, loosen, or add disclosure requirements around a nationally-defined trigger. And every fired rule writes its `reason_code` at the point of decision — never reconstructed later — which is the single discipline most commonly tested in a coverholder audit.

**What's built vs. pending:** the rules that depend on external data feeds — VV-01 (VIN decode), VV-02 (title history), DH-02 (household driver), PC-04 (producer verification) — are defined but not yet live, pending those vendor integrations. PC-02 (sanctions/OFAC) is the only `HARD_DECLINE_COMPLIANCE` rule and currently the platform's one real compliance exposure requiring a vendor/integration decision (see §13).

## 7. The rating engine

When an application clears the gates, pricing runs as a fixed chain. The reference chain (drawn from the consolidated rating framework) is a 12-step sequence:

1. National PD (physical damage) base rate — a function of vehicle class × agreed-value band, **not** state
2. × 14 core factors
3. × 14 coverage factors
4. → liability loss cost
5. + ALAE/ULAE (claim adjustment expense loads)
6. + catastrophe load = state cat load × ZIP-zone multiplier × cat-deductible credit
7. + risk margin
8. = **pure risk premium**
9. ÷ gross-up divisor → **technical premium**
10. ± underwriting judgment → **gross written premium (GWP)**
11. + fees + surplus-lines tax + stamping → **total due**

The structurally important idea here — and the one that resolves a long-standing question about the platform's data model — is that **state variation lives in the territory factor, not in the base rate.** Base rate is national (vehicle class × value band). Everything geographic — statewide territory factor, metro relativity, catastrophe zone by ZIP — is applied on top as territory-factor tiers. This is the correct structure: a national base-rate table is not a defect *provided* there is a real per-state (ideally sub-state) territory-factor dimension feeding it.

The platform today implements the single statewide territory factor per state. The finer tiers (metro relativity, ZIP-3 catastrophe zones) are designed and understood but not yet built into the ingestion path — that's the territory-dimension work in §13.

## 8. The state rating registry — the compliance boundary

The state rating registry (`state_rating_table_schema.json`) is the legal boundary the rating engine must stay inside. It holds **one record per state per effective-date version**, and each record defines what the engine is *permitted* to do in that state. It does not store the full proprietary rate manual — it stores the compliance metadata plus a pointer to where the filed manual lives, so the engine can validate itself before it prices anything.

How it's used at runtime: an application arrives with a garaging state; the registry is queried for that state's active record as of the rate-effective date; the rating engine cross-checks every variable it intends to use against the record's `approved_rating_variables`; **any variable not on the permitted list, or on `prohibited_variables`, is a hard stop, not a warning.** The quote then attaches a decision log citing which permitted variables drove the price.

What each record governs: filing status (prior-approval / file-and-use / etc.), approved and prohibited rating variables, credit-based-insurance-score permissions, gender-rating permission, territory basis, agreed-value rules, state-specific referral thresholds (DUI look-back, SR-22/FR-44), and an **AI-governance block**.

**The AI-governance block is the part that makes this platform defensible.** States are moving fast on algorithmic-fairness rules. NY DFS Circular Letter 2024-7 is currently the strictest bar — it requires insurers to show that AI / external-data systems don't proxy for protected classes, to keep explanatory documentation, to allow the regulator to review vendor tools, and to require vendor audits. The design decision was to **build the decision-log / explainability feature to NY's standard by default**, so every other state is a subset rather than a special case. Colorado (C.R.S. 10-3-1104.9) and the NAIC Model Bulletin adoptions (states like MA and HI) are handled by the same machinery.

States that fully ban credit-based insurance scoring for auto (CA, MA, HI, MI in the current sample set) are handled by the prohibited-variable hard stop — the engine cannot even see that variable for a vehicle garaged there.

## 9. Commission and profit commission

**Canonical structure (ADR 0042).** For the platform, the commission structure is: **25% total acquisition cost**, split **12.5% to Torque (the MGA)** and **12.5% to the broker**, leaving **75% of premium to capacity** — distributed across 10 named Lloyd's syndicates modeled at 10% each. (That even split is a modeling simplification; the actual signed-line weights differ in the program workbook.)

**Profit commission.** If the book performs, Torque earns a profit commission on a sliding scale keyed to the selected loss ratio. In the demo dataset, a ~0.56 loss ratio lands in Band 5 → a 10% profit commission.

**A reconciliation you must respect.** There are three different commission views floating around this project, and they do not reconcile without a note. They must never be shown side-by-side without one:

| Source | Combined commission cap | Net premium to panel |
|---|---|---|
| **ADR 0039** (earlier shipped platform view) | 30% | gross × 0.70 |
| **ADR 0042 / program workbook** (canonical) | 25% | gross × 0.75 |
| **Syndicate panel (workbook)** | 26.5–28.5% per syndicate (≈27.3% share-weighted) | — |

ADR 0042 is the canonical platform structure. The 30% / gross×0.70 figure from ADR 0039 and the 25% / gross×0.75 figure are *different framings that require a reconciliation note whenever both appear.* For any investor-facing surface, the commission waterfall detail stays off the slide entirely — it's an internal reconciliation, not a headline number.

**One more number-hygiene rule:** the operating-expense figure used in the program model (~$10.6M) is calibrated to a ~$60M plan GWP, **not** to the demo dataset's GWP. The two must never be combined in a single P&L without an explicit reconciliation note, or you'd be dividing a big-plan expense base by a small-demo revenue base and getting a nonsense ratio.

## 10. How the Odoo system works

Odoo is the **interface layer**, and one architectural rule governs everything about how it's wired in: **Odoo reads through database views only (ADR 0006).** It does not own the rating logic, it does not write rating data directly, and it does not bypass the stored procedures. The database is the system of record and the arbiter of how risks are priced and gated; Odoo is the human-facing surface on top of it.

Why Odoo at all: it's a free, self-hostable, open-source ERP with strong built-in document management, invoicing, and an extensible data model. For an MGA whose whole thesis is expense efficiency, avoiding a per-seat enterprise MGA-software contract (which run into thousands of dollars a month) while still getting document management, invoicing, and a customizable object model is exactly the right trade. The MGA-specific concept it doesn't do out of the box — splitting premium/risk/commission across capacity participants (quota share) — is precisely the custom piece the platform builds.

**What's been built into Odoo:**

- **Underwriter roster management (ADR 0038)** — who the underwriters are and what authority level each holds.
- **The underwriter override UI (ADR 0040)** — where a human reviews a referred application and confirms or overrides the pipeline's recommendation, all through views over the database.

**A known caveat, flagged honestly:** the override UI is currently granted to Odoo's `base.group_user` group for demo speed. Before any non-demo use it must be restricted to a dedicated `group_underwriting` security group. That's tracked work, not shipped (see §13).

## 11. What the platform produces — outputs and artifacts

The things Kent can actually pull and show. This is the "pull documents to show the output" half of the request.

- **Quotes** — priced offers, each carrying the variables that drove the price.
- **Decision logs** — per application, the record of which referral rules fired (with `reason_code`s) and which permitted variables drove the price. This is the audit and adverse-action artifact.
- **The canonical demo dataset (ADRs 0043/0045/0049)** — a frozen, hash-verified artifact representing a full synthetic book run through the platform: **10,500 submissions, 7,655 bound, $71.30M GWP**, with an **ultimate loss ratio of 0.5600** (total incurred policy-period losses ÷ bound GWP, per the ADR 0044 loss-model recalibration). The artifact is content-addressed by SHA-256 (`5a2be288360c84289225ee75ff782c947bcb6a014f7627cb16a7d45946789ac2`) so anyone can prove they're looking at the exact same dataset.
- **`snapshot.json`** — the read-only export the dashboard renders from, written by the exporter service off the demo DB.
- **The live investor dashboard** (`dashboard.ironcliffvertex.com`) — the rendered portfolio view.
- **The control panel** (`control.ironcliffvertex.com`) — drives the demo.
- **Bordereaux** — the structured premium/claims reports an MGA submits up to its syndicates; the templates exist in the program workbook and are the reporting artifact the whole data model is designed to feed.

**A precise note on the loss-ratio basis**, because it matters for anyone reading the number: the 0.5600 loss ratio is on an **ultimate incurred basis** — total incurred policy-period losses divided by bound GWP — as recalibrated and documented in ADR 0044. It is not a technical (pure-risk ÷ charged) figure. Stating the basis explicitly is the difference between a defensible number and a misleading one.

## 12. Evidence and verification — how to prove it works

This section answers Kent's sharpest question directly: *where is the evidence that any of this is doing anything?* The honest, useful answer is not "trust the manual" — it's **"here is exactly how to pull each proof yourself."** Every claim in this document maps to something verifiable.

**The ADR trail.** Every architectural and business-rule decision is captured as an Architecture Decision Record in `docs/decisions/` in the repo — ADRs 0001–0043. The manual's claims about *why* the system is shaped the way it is are all backed by a dated, reviewable ADR. Reading the ADR sequence is the fastest way to see the build actually happened, decision by decision.

**Git verification.** Commits are never trusted from a push exit code or a report — they're verified against the remote with `git ls-remote`. To confirm the build state:

```
git ls-remote origin refs/heads/main
git ls-remote origin refs/heads/demo/investor-preview
```

Last known reference points (re-verify — these are "last known," not live): `main` at `5ceebd1`; demo branch tip around `0ffb7d2`; demo build artifacts committed at `cce7299`. If those hashes match the remote, the build described here is the build that's deployed.

**CI verification.** The deploy pipeline runs on the self-hosted GitHub Actions runner on the VM. The correct way to verify a CI change is to confirm the **specific new step appears in the job's step list** — not merely that the job went green (a green run can skip the step you care about). Because the unauthenticated GitHub API is rate-limited, check run status either directly on the VM or via the Actions tab on github.com rather than scripting against `api.github.com` anonymously.

**The demo database.** The `10,500 submissions / 7,655 bound / $71.30M GWP` figures are not asserted — they're queryable. Point at the `luxauto_demo` database and count. The exporter service reads the same data into `snapshot.json`, so the dashboard and a direct DB query should agree. (The 26 name-only reattributions of ADR 0049 postdate the demo-DB load; counts and GWP are unaffected.)

**The canonical artifact hash.** The frozen dataset is content-addressed by SHA-256. Re-hash the artifact and compare — if it matches, you're provably looking at the same dataset the loss ratio and PC band were computed from.

**The live surfaces.** The dashboard and control panel are deployed and reachable (behind Entra auth) at their Cloudflare domains. They render off `snapshot.json`, which is produced by a read-only exporter that cannot mutate the database — so the dashboard is evidence of the data, not a hand-edited display.

The through-line: **claims in this manual → ADR that decided it → commit on the remote → running service or queryable data → rendered output.** Every link is independently checkable.

## 13. Honest limitations and open work

A manual that only lists what works isn't trustworthy. Here's what is *not* done, stated plainly.

**Data reality.** The platform holds **no real filed rating data for any state.** Even the CT row that exists is illustrative seed data (`serff_filing_tracking_number = 'TBD-ILLUSTRATIVE'`). The demo's CA/NY/TX/FL rows are explicitly synthetic (`DEMO-SYNTHETIC-` SERFF prefixes). Before any state can feed a live rating decision, its actual filed rate manual and current DOI bulletins have to be pulled and verified against the registry — that's a compliance step, not a coding step.

**Enrichment stubs.** Four referral rules are defined but not live because they depend on external data sources not yet integrated: VV-01 (VIN decode), VV-02 (title history), DH-02 (household driver check), PC-04 (producer verification).

**Sanctions.** PC-02 (OFAC/sanctions) is the only hard-decline compliance rule and currently a real compliance exposure — it needs a screening vendor/integration decision, not just backlog grooming.

**Odoo security group.** The underwriter override UI is on `base.group_user` for demo speed; it must move to a dedicated `group_underwriting` group before non-demo use.

**Audit-accuracy gap.** The `referral_overrides` table should snapshot the authorizing underwriter's authority level at insert time (a proposed `authorized_by_authority_level` column), so historical override rows freeze the authority as it was, not as it later becomes. Scoped, not built.

**Territory granularity.** `onboard_state()` supports exactly one statewide PD territory factor per state — no ZIP/county sub-state granularity — and `rating_base_rates` is national with no per-state ingestion path. The target three-tier geography (statewide factor → metro relativity → ZIP-3 cat zone) is designed but not built. This is the single most important item for the 50-state rollout.

**Softening / replay (Build 3).** The frozen artifact encodes a 12-month premium-softening trend as a per-month index. The live `create_quote()` RPC re-rates **without** softening. Build 3 must consume the artifact's premiums directly (rather than re-rating through the RPC), or softening must become a rating-input dimension — otherwise the live demo board would show un-softened premiums that contradict the softening story.

**Loss-ratio basis documentation.** Landed as ADR 0044 (loss-model recalibration); the 0.5600 figure is on the ultimate incurred ÷ bound-GWP basis described there.

**Renewal workflow.** Named but entirely undesigned — the largest remaining unscoped piece of the platform.

## Appendix B — glossary

- **MGA (Managing General Agent)** — an intermediary delegated authority to underwrite on an insurer's behalf; does not carry the risk itself.
- **Binder / binding authority** — the agreement that grants an MGA the right to bind coverage on the syndicates' behalf within defined limits.
- **Coverholder** — the Lloyd's term for an approved delegated underwriter; Torque operates as one.
- **Capacity** — the syndicates providing the balance-sheet that actually carries the risk.
- **Bordereaux** — structured premium/claims reports the MGA submits up to its capacity.
- **GWP** — gross written premium.
- **Loss ratio (technical/rater basis)** — pure risk premium ÷ charged GWP (distinct from incurred ÷ earned).
- **Profit commission** — a share of underwriting profit paid to the MGA on a sliding scale by loss ratio.
- **Referral** — routing an application to a human (or halting for information) instead of auto-quoting.
- **SECURITY DEFINER** — a PostgreSQL function that runs with its definer's privileges; used to make rating logic behave consistently regardless of caller.
- **SERFF** — the interstate System for Electronic Rate and Form Filing.
